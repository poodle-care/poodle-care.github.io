Brushing & clipping

How Long Does Poodle Grooming Really Take? Weekly Hours by Clip Style, from Kennel Clip to Show Continental

Published 2026-11-01

If you have ever wondered how long Poodle grooming really takes once the puppy fluff turns into an adult coat, the honest answer depends far more on the clip you choose than on the dog. A short kennel clip can live on well under an hour a week of your time, while a Poodle in full show Continental can eat four hours or more every single week. This guide puts real owner numbers next to each clip so you can pick a coat that fits your life, not someone else's.

Quick answer: Owners who groom their own Standard Poodles report roughly 45-60 minutes a week for a kennel or sporting clip, about 1.5 hours a week for a Miami, around 2 hours a week for a modified Continental and 3.5-5 hours a week for a full show Continental. Drying is the biggest single block of time. Paying a groomer for the cut removes the longest sessions, but the brushing between visits still lands on you.

Every household is different, so start with your own dog. Pick a clip, size and who does the work, then replace any of the example minutes with your own timings. The calculator turns them into weekly, monthly and yearly hours and tells you which clip fits the time you actually have.

One thing jumps out as soon as you play with the numbers: brushing is rarely the big cost. The hours hide in the bath-and-dry session, and especially in drying. A Poodle coat is dried straight with a strong dryer and a brush before it can be clipped neatly, and one owner in the thread below says a complete blow dry on a Standard takes at least two hours.

How long does Poodle grooming take each week by clip?

From a kennel clip to a show Continental, owner-reported home grooming for a Standard Poodle runs from under an hour to about five hours a week. The table below turns each owner's routine into a weekly figure so the clips can be compared on one scale.

The numbers come from a single, recent and unusually specific source: an October 2026 thread on r/StandardPoodles where a professional groomer asked how much time owners realistically spend grooming, and owners answered with minutes and hours for their own dogs. What the pros and the breed club say about time

Owners are not just being slow. The Paragon School of Pet Grooming notes on its blog that proficient pet stylists can finish a small or medium dog in about an hour, but that Standard Poodles take between 2 1/2 and 3 1/2 hours for bath, dry and haircut. Even in competition, the Poodle Club of America's 2025 Just Groom a Poodle event gave competitors 1 hour 45 minutes for a Toy, 2 hours for a Miniature and 2 hours 30 minutes for a Standard, and that was with feet and faces allowed to be clipped beforehand.

Why does a show Continental take hours every week?

A Continental keeps a full mane of long coat that has to be washed, dried straight and protected every week, on top of shaved areas that need re-clipping. The long hair is the cost: one owner with a full show coat described about an hour of bathing, a few hours of drying and fluffing, then another hour of banding or wrapping, every week.

The owner of a white Standard in full show Continental gave a similar breakdown. A simple maintenance bath, meaning bathe, brush and dry, clip the clipped areas and band, takes about 3.5 hours and happens every week or every other week. A full groom with scissoring takes about 5 hours every four weeks, and is sometimes split over two days. Bands get redone about once between baths, at 20-45 minutes a time.

Where a show coat saves time

Interestingly, the same show-coat owners do almost no daily brushing. The coat is brushed while it is dried and kept in bands, so the daily job is fixing a band here and there. The time is concentrated in one long weekly session instead of spread across the week. Whether that suits you depends on whether you can protect a three-to-five-hour block every weekend.

Where do the grooming hours actually go?

For most pet clips the hours split three ways: brushing between baths, the bath-and-dry session, and clipping or scissoring. Drying is usually the largest single piece, which is why a better drying routine is the fastest way to win time back.

Brushing

VCA Animal Hospitals says brushing and combing every other day is necessary to prevent matting, and rates Poodle grooming needs at the top of its scale. In practice owners report 5 minutes a session for a short kennel clip and 10-20 minutes for a Miami with more leg and topknot hair. One owner of a Toy with a short coat brushes for about 5 minutes, rising to 7-10 minutes when the coat is longer. Knowing where Poodle mats form for each clip lets you spend those minutes on the ears, armpits and collar line instead of brushing the whole dog evenly.

Bathing and drying

The kennel-clip owner's numbers show the shape of a session: about 45 minutes to wash, condition and towel dry, letting the coat air dry to about 85 percent, then about 30 minutes of blow drying. Another owner said a complete blow dry takes at least two hours. A Miami owner reports a full bath and dry at about 1.5 hours, rising to 2-3 hours once the coat is grown into a modern or modified Continental in winter.

Clipping and scissoring

Shaving down a short clip and trimming the head and tail took one couple about an hour. Setting new lines for a fresh style on a coat with plenty of length can take 5-6 hours for a full groom, while cleaning up an existing summer Miami takes 2-3 hours start to finish. If you only tidy the face, feet and sanitary area between salon visits, a small cordless trimmer keeps those jobs short; our Wahl Bravura2 review for Poodle face and feet work times those sessions.

How much time does a groomer save you?

A groomer removes the longest sessions, the full bath, dry and haircut, but not the brushing in between. VCA notes that most owners have their Poodles professionally clipped about every six weeks and that most choose a sporting clip, fairly short all over.

The owner thread shows what is left at home. One Poodle in a kennel clip with a mohawk and a tail puff goes to the groomer every six weeks and needs about five minutes with a slicker at home. Another, in a pattern clip, also goes every six weeks but gets a full bath and brush-out at home every week, and stays mat free without brushing between those baths. A third owner who uses a groomer still bathes every two to four weeks and does a little brushing every day.

Count the trip, too

Owner time with a groomer is not zero. Driving, drop-off and pick-up add up across a year, which is why the calculator has a field for the groomer trip. Put in your real round trip and see whether a home bath or a salon visit costs you less time.

Toy, Miniature or Standard: does size change the clock?

Yes, but less than you might expect. Bathing, drying and clipping get shorter on a smaller dog, while brushing time stays similar because the same hotspots need the same attention.

The Poodle Club of America's competition limits give a fair ratio: a Toy got 70 percent and a Miniature 80 percent of a Standard's time for the same job. The calculator uses that ratio for bath, dry and haircut minutes on smaller sizes and leaves brushing alone. Owners in the thread back that up: a Toy owner with a short coat does a weekly 45-minute wash and dry with breaks for a dog still learning to like it, and brushes for 5-10 minutes a day, while a Miniature owner budgets about an hour per bath plus roughly 20 minutes for bands.

Age matters more than size for a while

The coat change from puppy fluff to adult coat is when brushing time jumps, because the two coat types tangle together. If your dog is under 18 months, check the Poodle puppy coat change timeline and expect more minutes than these adult benchmarks for a few months.

Build a grooming week that fits your schedule

The right clip is the one you can keep up with on your worst week, not your best. Use these steps to tailor a routine to your own dog, your calendar and your budget.

  1. Time one honest session. At the next bath, note when you start and when the dog is dry, and the same for one brushing session. Put those minutes into the calculator instead of the example values.
  2. Set your weekly ceiling. Enter the minutes you can reliably give, including busy months. The calculator lists every owner routine that fits that number.
  3. Choose the clip from the ceiling, not the photo. If you have under an hour, a kennel or sporting clip is the realistic choice. Between 1.5 and 2 hours opens a Miami or modified Continental. A show Continental needs a fixed long block every week.
  4. Decide who does the long sessions. If drying and scissoring push you over your ceiling, switch the calculator to a groomer and keep only brushing and baths at home.
  5. Put the sessions on a calendar. Fixed days work better than "when it looks scruffy". Our free Poodle grooming planner gives you a printable calendar for brushing, baths and salon visits.
  6. Adjust by season. Some owners keep a shorter coat in winter to cut brushing, while others leave more length for warmth. If you walk in snow, the guide to winter clip length and snowballs shows how leg length changes cleanup time after walks.
  7. Review after a month. Compare the calculator's monthly figure with what really happened. If you missed sessions, shorten the clip before mats make the next groom longer.

Common time traps that make Poodle grooming take longer

Most "grooming takes forever" stories come from a few avoidable habits rather than from the breed itself.

  • Skipping both brushing and regular baths. Owners who barely brush between baths make up for it with weekly or fortnightly baths and a thorough blow dry. Pick one system, brushing or frequent baths, and stick to it, because skipping both is how mats start.
  • Rushing the dry. One owner notes you hardly have to brush between baths if you blow dry properly and bathe every one to two weeks. The kennel-clip couple lets the coat air dry to about 85 percent before blow drying, which works on a short clip; a longer clip needs the full blow dry.
  • Choosing a show clip for a pet schedule. A Continental needs a weekly multi-hour block. Without it, the coat mats and the dog ends up shaved anyway.
  • Doing the whole groom in one go with a nervous dog. One show-coat owner splits a full groom over two days: bath, dry and rough bands one day, clipping and scissoring the next. Shorter sessions are easier on both of you.
  • Forgetting the small weekly jobs. Ears and nails take minutes a week but add up; include them as weekly extras so your total is honest.
  • Judging by a doodle's coat. One owner pointed out that Poodles tend to have easier coats than doodles, so a doodle's grooming time is not a fair preview.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many hours a week does a Poodle need for grooming?

Owners who groom at home report about 45-60 minutes a week for a short kennel clip, about 1.5 hours for a Miami, 2-3 hours for a modified Continental and 3.5-5 hours for a full show Continental. A groomer reduces this to brushing and any home baths.

How often should a Poodle be brushed?

VCA Animal Hospitals says brushing and combing every other day is necessary to prevent matting. Short clips need about 5 minutes a session; longer pet clips 10-20 minutes.

How long does a groomer take to groom a Standard Poodle?

The Paragon School of Pet Grooming puts a Standard Poodle at 2 1/2 to 3 1/2 hours for a proficient groomer, including bath, dry and haircut. Small and medium dogs take about an hour.

How often do Poodles need a professional groom?

VCA notes that most owners have their Poodles professionally clipped about every six weeks. Owners in the 2026 thread also mention four-week cycles for full cuts on longer clips.

What is the lowest-maintenance Poodle clip?

A short kennel or sporting clip. One owner reports about 5 minutes of brushing every other day and a 2-2.5-hour full groom every 4-6 weeks, about 45-60 minutes a week in total.

Does a Toy Poodle take less time to groom than a Standard?

Bathing, drying and clipping take less time. At the Poodle Club of America's grooming competition, a Toy got 1 hour 45 minutes against 2 hours 30 minutes for a Standard. Brushing time stays similar.

The bottom line

How long Poodle grooming takes is mostly a choice you make when you pick the clip. Real owners spend under an hour a week on a kennel clip, about an hour and a half on a Miami, around two hours on a modified Continental and three and a half to five hours on a show Continental, with drying taking the biggest share. Time one honest session, set a weekly ceiling, and let the calculator show you which clip fits the life you actually have.
