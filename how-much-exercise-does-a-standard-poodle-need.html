How Much Exercise Does a Standard Poodle Need? Daily Needs by Age

Published 2026-05-22

A Standard Poodle needs real exercise every day: not a stroll round the block, but a long walk or jog, or several vigorous games, plus something that makes it think. That is the short version of what the American Kennel Club breed profile and the VCA Animal Hospitals breed guide both say. What nobody can honestly give you is one magic number of minutes that fits every Poodle from eight weeks to twelve years. This guide shows what the sources actually agree on, where the popular rules come from, and how to build a day that suits your own dog.

Quick answer: Plan for at least 30 minutes of hard, heart-pumping activity every day for an adult Standard Poodle (running, swimming, fetch), on top of ordinary walks, and add a daily brain game such as a puzzle feeder or a short training session. The 30-minute floor comes from the ASPCA's enrichment advice; most Standards happily do more. Puppies get short play, walk and training sessions with plenty of rest, not long runs. Toy and Miniature Poodles need the same mix in smaller doses.

What do the breed experts actually say?

The AKC describes Poodles of any size as very active dogs that need good exercise every day to match their high energy. It points to swimming, retrieving games and jogs or long walks with their owner, which fits the breed's origin as a water retriever. VCA's breed guide is more concrete: a long walk or jog, or several vigorous games, every day will meet a Standard Poodle's exercise needs, and games and tricks provide the mental exercise it also needs.

Notice what is missing: neither source gives a minute count. That is honest. A two-year-old Standard bred from working lines and a mellow seven-year-old from show lines are both "Standard Poodles", and their needs are not the same. The figures you see repeated online (60 minutes, 90 minutes, two hours) are rules of thumb, not measurements.

The firmest number comes from the ASPCA. In its advice on dogs that struggle when left alone, it suggests at least 30 minutes of aerobic activity, such as running and swimming, every day, ideally right before you leave. That is a floor for a big, active dog, not a ceiling, and it is a useful anchor for a Standard.

Why a Poodle needs brain work as well as legs

Poodles were bred to work with people. The AKC's notes on mental stimulation warn that purpose-bred dogs without the brain exercise they need will create their own projects, and probably not ones you would like. In a Poodle that can look like shredded post, raided bins or barking at every sound. The PDSA exercise guide calls brain games a great addition to any dog's routine, and especially important for breeds that need more mental stimulation or on days when a dog gets less exercise than usual. It suggests feeding puzzles and snuffle mats.

Easy ways to add brain work without extra time:

  • Feed one meal from a puzzle feeder or scatter it in the grass to "hunt", as the ASPCA suggests.
  • Do three minutes of trick training before each meal.
  • Hide a toy in another room and ask your Poodle to find it.
  • Change your walking route. PDSA notes that a slow, meandering walk with time to sniff and explore is good for a dog's mental wellbeing.

Exercise by age

Puppies

The most quoted puppy rule is "five minutes of exercise per month of age". PDSA, the UK veterinary charity, is blunt about it: there is no scientific evidence behind the rule, and although it might work for some puppies, it is not appropriate for most. What PDSA does recommend is play, short walks and training, with rest whenever the puppy seems tired, and keeping high-impact activity for later, once the puppy has finished growing. Because excitable puppies rarely tell you they are tired, PDSA suggests clipping the lead on now and then to enforce a break.

For a Poodle puppy, that means several short sessions a day rather than one long outing: a walk round the block, a game of tug, a few minutes of training, then a nap. Leave jogging, bike runs, agility jumps and long hikes until your vet or breeder confirms your dog has stopped growing. Standards take longer to mature than Toys and Minis.

The same months are also when the coat changes, so short daily handling sessions double as grooming practice. See the puppy coat change timeline for what to expect.

Adults

An adult Standard is the athlete of the family. Aim for the ASPCA's 30 minutes of hard, aerobic exercise as a minimum, add walks where the dog gets to sniff and explore, and finish with a brain game. Many Standards are happy with an hour or more of activity on top of that. Toy and Miniature Poodles need the same three ingredients; the hard part is simply shorter, and a vigorous game of fetch in the garden or hallway can cover it.

Seniors

Older Poodles still need daily walks and things to think about, but intensity drops. Let your dog set the pace, switch long outings for two shorter ones, and lean on scent games and puzzle feeders. If your senior suddenly lags behind, stiffens up after walks or no longer wants to go, ask your vet before you change the routine.

Signs your Poodle needs more (or different) exercise

Behaviour tells you more than a stopwatch. These are common signs that a Poodle's day is missing something:

  • It cannot settle in the evening and paces or nudges you constantly.
  • It chews, digs or shreds things when bored.
  • It barks at every small noise.
  • It gets "zoomies" indoors every night.
  • It pulls hard on the lead for the first ten minutes of every walk.

Not every problem is fixed with more running, though. A dog that is calm while you are home but panics within minutes of you leaving may be dealing with separation distress rather than boredom. The ASPCA explains how to tell the two apart, and our guide to Standard Poodles and time alone walks through it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many minutes of exercise does a Standard Poodle need?

There is no official minute count from the AKC or VCA. The ASPCA suggests at least 30 minutes of aerobic activity daily for active dogs, and most adult Standards need ordinary walks and a brain game on top of that.

Is a walk enough for a Standard Poodle?

Usually not on its own. VCA recommends a long walk or jog, or several vigorous games, every day, plus games and tricks for mental exercise.

Does the 5-minutes-per-month rule apply to Poodle puppies?

PDSA says there is no scientific evidence behind the rule and that it is not appropriate for most puppies. Use short play, walk and training sessions with plenty of rest instead.

When can my Poodle start jogging with me?

Only once it has finished growing. Standards mature later than Toys and Minis, so ask your vet or breeder to confirm before you start.

Do Toy and Miniature Poodles need as much exercise?

They need the same mix of hard play, walks and brain work, with shorter sessions. The AKC describes Poodles of every size as very active.

Is swimming good exercise for Poodles?

Yes. The AKC calls swimming great exercise for Poodles and says most love the water. VCA notes a full coat can weigh a dog down, so a shorter clip helps.

Can mental games replace physical exercise?

Not completely, but PDSA says brain games are especially useful on days when a dog gets less exercise than usual.

The bottom line

Give an adult Standard Poodle at least 30 minutes of real, hard exercise every day, add walks with time to explore, and finish with something to think about. Keep puppies to short sessions with rest, let seniors set the pace, and watch your dog's behaviour rather than chasing a number.
