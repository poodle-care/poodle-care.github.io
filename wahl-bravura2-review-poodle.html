Wahl Bravura2 Review 2026: Clipping a Poodle's Face, Feet and Topknot at Home with the New Bravura2 vs the Original Bravura Lithium

Published 2026-10-28

If you clip your own Poodle between salon visits, the jobs that never wait are the face, the feet and the clean line where the topknot starts. That is exactly what a small 5-in-1 clipper is for, and in March 2026 Wahl replaced one of the most common ones with the Bravura2. This Wahl Bravura2 review looks at it the way a Poodle owner would: does the new model make face and feet sessions easier, or is the original Bravura Lithium still enough?

Quick answer: For a Poodle owner who only tidies the face, feet and sanitary area, the original Bravura Lithium's 90 minutes of cordless time already covers several sessions per charge. The Bravura2 earns its place if you groom more than one dog, also run guide combs over the body, or want a second, faster speed and an on-screen battery count in minutes. If you already own a Bravura that still runs well, a fresh 5-in-1 blade is the cheapest upgrade.

Bravura2 vs original Bravura Lithium: what actually changed?

According to Wahl's launch announcement from 23 March 2026, the Bravura2 uses a brushless rotary motor with two speeds, 6,000 and 8,000 RPM, runs up to four hours cordless, and gets up to 2.5 hours of use from a 45-minute USB-C quick charge. Its new 5-in-1 Diamond Pro blade has a diamond-like carbon (DLC) coating that Wahl says runs cooler, stays sharp longer and resists corrosion, with five cutting lengths from 0.7 to 3.0 mm. The housing is textured for grip, the power button has a travel lock, and a small screen shows the speed, the battery life in minutes and blade maintenance reminders.

The original is a simpler tool. Wahl Canada's listing for the Bravura Lithium gives up to 90 minutes of cordless runtime, a 60-minute full charge and the ability to clip while charging, with a 5-in-1 fine blade covering #9, #10, #15, #30 and #40 and an LCD battery level icon.

1. Wahl Bravura2: best for multi-dog homes and body trims

The Bravura2 is the one to buy if your home sessions are long. Four hours of cordless time covers a face, feet and sanitary tidy on several Poodles, or a full body run with a guide comb, without reaching for the cord. The minute counter on the screen is a small thing that matters on a wriggly dog: you know before you start the second cheek whether you have enough charge.

For Poodle faces the two speeds are the more interesting change. The lower setting is the one to use near the eyes and lips and on a dog that is still learning to accept the buzz. The higher setting is there for the feet and the thick, curly body coat.

Pros

  • Up to 240 minutes cordless, plus corded use.
  • Two speeds, so you can slow down for the face.
  • Battery shown in minutes, with blade maintenance reminders.
  • Fast USB-C top-up if you forgot to charge it.

Cons

  • New in 2026, so there is little long-term owner feedback yet.
  • Wahl does not say whether the Diamond Pro blade fits the original Bravura, so check before you mix parts.

2. Wahl Bravura Lithium: still enough for face and feet

The original remains a very good Poodle tool for one simple reason: the jobs it is best at are short. Ninety minutes of cordless time is more than most owners spend on a face, four feet and a sanitary trim, and the clipper keeps working while it charges if you run over. Its 5-in-1 blade covers the lengths that matter for Poodle feet and faces, from #9 down to #40, with a lever instead of blade swaps.

The six guide combs in the Canadian kit are also handy if you keep a short body coat between salon visits. If you find it discounted, it is the value pick.

Pros

  • A Wahl educator calls the Bravura "a favorite in the industry" in the Bravura2 launch release.
  • Covers #9 to #40 on one blade.
  • Six guide combs in the Canadian kit.
  • Can clip while charging.

Cons

  • 90 minutes cordless is tight for body work or several dogs.
  • Battery shown only as an icon, not in minutes.

3. A new 5-in-1 blade: the upgrade for owners who already have a Bravura

If your original Bravura still holds a charge but has started to pull on the feet or leave lines on the face, it is worth trying a fresh blade before replacing the whole clipper. Wahl, which calls itself the inventor of the 5-in-1 blade and the maker of the Arco and Bravura, sells replacement 5-in-1 blades; check the part number for your model before you buy. It does not add runtime or a second speed, but for a single Poodle on a face and feet routine it can make an old clipper feel new for far less than either clipper.

Pros

  • Cheapest way to fix pulling and uneven lines.
  • No new charger, stand or learning curve.

Cons

  • Same 90-minute battery and single display.
  • Will not help if the battery itself is worn out.

Clipping a Poodle's face with either Bravura

Start on a clean, fully brushed-out dog. Daryl Conner's Poodle trim basics at Groomer to Groomer notes that clipping clean, dry hair helps blades last longer and dramatically reduces clipper irritation. If the coat is knotted around the ears, sort that first using the hotspot routine in our guide to where Poodles mat first.

  1. Set the face line. Clip a line from the inside corner of the ear to the outside corner of the eye on each side. Everything in front of it is face; everything behind it belongs to the topknot and ears.
  2. Do the throat. Clip against the grain from the larynx area up to the line under the ears.
  3. Clear the muzzle and flews. Stretch the lip gently and pay attention to the flews, the small fold on each side of the lower lip.
  4. Finish the eyes slowly. Clip carefully under the lower lid and around the eye corners. On the Bravura2, this is where the lower speed earns its keep.

If your dog is new to clipping, keep the first sessions short and stop on a good minute. Puppies going through the coat change in our Poodle puppy coat change timeline are often getting their first full face clips at the same time, so expect a few practice runs.

Clipping Poodle feet

Feet are where a 5-in-1 blade shines, because you can shorten the setting for the toes without stopping to change blades. Conner's guide says most dogs tolerate a #15 blade length on the feet and many do fine with a #30. Work against the grain, clip up and away from the large pad, and keep the clipper line where the foot bends. With the right tool and a good technique, she says, four feet take a professional just 5 to 8 minutes; plan for longer the first few times.

If a spot keeps getting nicked or your dog pulls away, set a shorter length and only touch up that area rather than pushing harder. In winter, feet clipped close also pick up fewer ice balls, which our guide to Poodle snowballs covers in more detail.

What about the topknot?

Neither Bravura shapes a topknot for you. The clipper's job is the boundary: once the face line from ear to eye is clean, the topknot has a clear edge to work from. The topknot itself is brushed out, then shaped with scissors and a comb, so a newer clipper changes nothing there. What it can change is how relaxed your dog is by the time you get to the top of the head, which is another argument for the slower setting near the face.

Which one fits a Toy, Miniature or Standard Poodle?

Size matters less for face and feet work than you might think: a Toy's feet are smaller, but the steps are the same. It matters more once you add the body. Wikipedia's Poodle entry notes that pet Poodles are most often clipped evenly over the whole body with the face and paws cut shorter, and that owners can expect a groom every four to eight weeks. If you plan to do that even body length yourself on a Standard, the extra runtime of the Bravura2 is the difference between finishing cordless and working on a cord. For a Toy or Miniature on a face and feet schedule, the original is plenty.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Wahl Bravura2 worth it over the original Bravura for a Poodle?

Only if you need its extras. For face, feet and sanitary trims on one Poodle, the original's 90 minutes of cordless time is enough. The Bravura2 is worth it for several dogs, body work with guide combs, or if you want two speeds and a battery display in minutes.

What blade setting should I use on Poodle feet?

Groomer and writer Daryl Conner says most dogs tolerate a #15 length on the feet and many do fine with a #30. Start longer on a new or sensitive dog and only go shorter on areas that need it.

How long does the Bravura2 battery last?

Wahl states up to four hours of cordless runtime, and a 45-minute USB-C quick charge gives up to 2.5 hours of use. It can also run corded.

Can I use the Bravura2 blade on my old Bravura?

Wahl's launch material does not say. Check the Diamond Pro blade's fitment with Wahl or the seller before you buy it for an older clipper.

Can I clip a Poodle's topknot with a Bravura?

You use the clipper for the face line from the inside corner of the ear to the outside corner of the eye, which gives the topknot its edge. The topknot itself is brushed and scissored, not clipped.

Should I bathe my Poodle before clipping the face and feet?

Yes. Clipping clean, dry, brushed-out hair helps the blade last longer and reduces clipper irritation on the skin.

The bottom line

This Wahl Bravura2 review comes down to how long you clip. For a Poodle owner who keeps the face, feet and topknot line tidy between salon visits, the original Bravura Lithium, or a new blade on the one you already own, does the job well. The Bravura2 is the better clipper on paper, with four hours of runtime, two speeds and a battery display in minutes, and it is the right buy for multi-dog homes and owners who also trim the body. Use the planner above with your own timings and let the numbers decide.
