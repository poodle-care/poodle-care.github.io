Coat changes

Poodle Puppy Coat Change Timeline: Birth to 18 Months

Published 2026-06-02

Poodle puppies are born with a soft, fluffy coat that later gives way to the denser, curlier adult coat. Most guides give the general dog timeline of four to six months for this switch. Poodles tend to be late. The coat change is the stage where many owners meet their first serious mats, because new curly hair grows in underneath the soft puppy hair and the two tangle together near the skin. This timeline shows what usually happens month by month, why Standards are often later than Toys and Minis, and how to change brushing and grooming at each stage.

Quick answer: The AKC says puppies in general lose the puppy coat between 4 and 6 months, though it can start as early as 12 weeks or wait until about a year. Poodles are usually at the late end. Grooming site OodleLife puts the Standard Poodle change at around 9 to 16 months, with Toys and Minis earlier, at around 9 months. Once the change starts, brush and comb down to the skin every day. That is when mats form fastest.

What actually changes in a Poodle's coat?

The AKC explains that puppies are born with a single coat of soft, fluffy fur, which naturally gives way to an adult coat that is usually thicker and stiffer. Breeds with single coats grow their own distinctive adult coat as they shed the puppy one. In a Poodle, that adult coat is the dense, tight curl the breed is known for. Wikipedia's Poodle entry describes it as a thick, curly coat with harsh hair.

Because the adult curls trap loose hair instead of letting it fall, a Poodle in the middle of the change does not leave much hair on the floor. The puppy hair that is coming out stays in the coat and wraps around the new curls. That is why the coat change shows up as matting rather than shedding. The AKC breed profile warns that if a full-coated Poodle is not brushed and combed completely to the skin, the hair mats near the roots and has to be shaved off.

The Standard and Toy/Miniature windows come from OodleLife's coat change guide, which says Standards usually lose the puppy coat at around 9 to 16 months and that it can take up to three months to finish. It also says Toys and Minis tend to start earlier, at around nine months, but can take as long as nine months before the adult coat comes in. The AKC's broader range of 12 weeks to about a year for dogs in general is a reminder that individual puppies vary.

Birth to 8 months: the puppy coat

This is the easy part for the coat and the important part for the puppy. The AKC points out that regular grooming during the change from puppy to adult coat also gets your dog used to being handled all over its body, which matters for breeds that will be groomed for life. If you plan to use a professional groomer, the AKC suggests starting while the puppy is young and finding one who is gentle and patient.

Practical goals for these months:

  • Brush gently every day for a minute or two, with a treat at the end, as the AKC recommends.
  • Get your puppy used to the comb behind the ears, under the legs and around the feet.
  • Book a first puppy groom. OodleLife says most Poodle owners book it between 11 and 16 weeks and recommends one before eight months. The first visit is usually just a bath, face, feet, a sanitary tidy and nails.
  • Let your puppy hear a clipper running before it ever touches the coat. If you plan to clip at home, our Wahl Bravura2 review compares two quiet cordless options for face and feet.

Around 9 months: the change begins

The first sign is usually texture. Part the coat and look at the base of the hair: if it is tighter and curlier there while the ends are still soft and fluffy, the adult coat is coming in. Many owners notice the change only when a brush session that used to take ten minutes suddenly finds knots everywhere.

From this point on, the method matters more than the brush. Surface brushing glides over the top and leaves the knots underneath. Line combing, where you part the coat and comb a small section from the skin out, is the technique that keeps a changing coat manageable. Our Poodle matting hotspots map shows where to start (behind the ears, armpits, collar and harness lines, tail base) and how each spot changes with the clip.

Peak change: when mats come back overnight

This is the hard stretch. OodleLife recommends brushing daily once the puppy starts blowing its coat, using a comb or slicker brush to remove the dead hair, and says bathing a little more often during this stage is fine. Here is a routine that fits into a normal day:

Always brush out mats before a bath, not after. Water tightens mats, and a coat that air-dries while knotted felts quickly. If the coat gets ahead of you, there is no shame in asking the groomer for a short, even clip until the adult coat has come in. The AKC notes that most pet owners keep Poodles in a shorter trim anyway, and VCA says most people choose a short sporting clip.

Winter adds a twist: snow and ice cling to a changing coat. The Poodle snowballs guide covers clip length and boots for the cold months.

The adult coat: setting a lifelong routine

Once the coat is even from root to tip, the change is over and you can settle into a routine. The AKC says that unless you keep your Poodle in a short trim, you will need to brush it daily to prevent matting, and that many owners take their dog to a professional groomer every four to six weeks for a bath, grooming and a nail trim. VCA puts the usual interval at every six weeks, and Wikipedia gives a range of four to eight weeks.

Colour can change too

The AKC notes that some dogs grow an adult coat in a different colour or pattern from their puppy coat. If your Poodle's colour shifts, the coat change is usually when you first see it, because the new hair at the roots shows the adult shade while the fluffy ends still show the puppy one. Your breeder can tell you what to expect from your puppy's line.

Common mistakes during the coat change

  • Brushing only the surface. The top looks fluffy while felted mats build at the skin.
  • Bathing a matted coat. Water tightens mats. Brush out first.
  • Waiting for the first full groom until the change is over. The groomer can only shave a coat that is already matted. Early, regular visits avoid that.
  • Using only a slicker brush. Finish with a metal comb. If the comb catches, the knot is still there.
  • Making sessions a fight. Short, daily sessions with treats beat one long battle a week. Your Poodle will be groomed for life, so its attitude matters.

Frequently Asked Questions

At what age do Poodles lose their puppy coat?

Later than most breeds. The AKC gives 4 to 6 months for dogs in general, and OodleLife says Standard Poodles usually change at around 9 to 16 months, with Toys and Minis starting at around 9 months.

How long does the Poodle coat change last?

OodleLife says it can take up to three months for a Standard to finish the change, while Toys and Minis can take as long as nine months before the adult coat comes in.

Why is my Poodle puppy suddenly matting so much?

New curly adult hair is growing in under the soft puppy hair, and the loose puppy hair wraps around it instead of falling out. Daily combing to the skin is the fix.

Do Poodle puppies shed?

A little, mostly during the change. The shed hair stays trapped in the curls, so you find it as mats rather than on the floor.

When should a Poodle puppy have its first groom?

OodleLife says most owners book it between 11 and 16 weeks and recommends one before eight months. It is usually a gentle bath, face, feet, sanitary tidy and nails.

Should I clip my Poodle short during the coat change?

If you cannot keep up with daily combing, a short, even clip is kinder than repeated de-matting. The AKC notes most pet owners keep Poodles in a shorter trim.

How often does an adult Poodle need grooming?

The AKC says many owners use a groomer every four to six weeks; VCA says every six weeks; Wikipedia gives four to eight weeks.

The bottom line

Expect your Poodle's puppy coat to change later than the general four-to-six-month rule, often around 9 to 16 months for a Standard. Use the puppy months to teach calm grooming, switch to daily line combing as soon as the roots tighten, and settle into a four-to-six-week grooming routine once the adult coat is in.
